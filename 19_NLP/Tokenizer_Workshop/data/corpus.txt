Natural language processing begins with tokenization. A tokenizer splits running text into words, sentences, or smaller units called tokens. Careful tokenization decides everything that follows, because bad tokens produce bad statistics.
Normalization comes next: lowercasing, unicode cleanup, and whitespace collapsing keep the analysis reproducible. The same sentence written twice must always produce the same list of tokens, run after run.
Stopwords are the tiny glue words of a language. Words like the, of, and, and to appear everywhere, so removing them leaves the content words that actually carry meaning.
Stemming reduces inflected forms to a common root. Running, ran, and runner are related, and a simple stemmer folds cats to cat and studies to study with a handful of suffix rules.
N-grams capture local word order. Bigrams like state of the art remember neighbouring words that unigrams throw away, which is why spell checkers study them closely.
Token statistics describe a corpus at a glance: total tokens, unique types, average length, and the most frequent words. Type-token ratios tell us whether a vocabulary is rich or repetitive.
In 2025, text pipelines still avoid downloads whenever possible. A workshop that ships its own corpus and stopwords needs no network, no api keys, and no model files to run.
Regex patterns do the heavy lifting. A pattern like a-z finds letter runs while apostrophes and hyphens glue related fragments together, for example don't and state-of-the-art.
Curators of dictionaries read thousands of sentences daily. They study usage, mark rare forms, and publish notes so editors can quickly verify tricky words.
Deterministic tools are testable tools. When every step is fixed and seeded, running the demo twice yields identical counts, and regressions are caught before they ship.
The pipelined stages never mutate the raw text. Normalization builds a new string, tokenization reads it, and stemming maps each token independently, so parallel workers stay consistent.
Short words matter too. Of, to, and it are removed as stopwords, yet at, in, and on show how tiny tokens still shape sentence rhythm when they are kept.
